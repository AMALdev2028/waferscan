How a wafer gets its verdict

Most wafers are decided by one fast, calibrated network. Only the unsure ones pay for the full ensemble, and only the still-unsure ones reach a person.

  1. Image to die grid

    A rendered WM-811K map or an optical wafer photo becomes a grid of dies: off-wafer, pass or fail. A photo with no periodic die grid is rejected, not guessed.

  2. Fast CNN

    One temperature-calibrated CNN in ONNX Runtime answers in about 2 ms. If its confidence clears the learned threshold (0.991), the wafer is decided here — 86.8% of wafers.

  3. Stacked ensemble

    The rest go to five CNNs plus a morphology gradient-boosting model, combined by a stacked meta-model.

  4. Shape check and decision

    Morphology rules check that the predicted pattern fits how the failing dies are laid out. Confident and consistent: auto-accept. Otherwise: human review.

What it can't do yet

Known limits, stated plainly so nobody trusts it further than the evidence goes.

  • Trained on a curated, class-balanced subset of WM-811K. Accuracy on the full imbalanced dataset or another fab's products must be measured separately.
  • WM-811K labels are sometimes ambiguous (Loc vs Edge-Loc). Unsure or shape-inconsistent wafers go to human review instead of being guessed.
  • Root-cause rankings are statistical associations plus engineering priors, not proof of causation.
  • There is no out-of-distribution check yet: a random-noise image can be read as a die grid and labelled None.
